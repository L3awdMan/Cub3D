Module 15 · Correction defense

Now sit in the corrector's chair. Can you defend every system?

At a 42 evaluation the corrector won't ask you to recite the subject — they'll point at a behaviour and ask "why does it do that, and where?" This is a gauntlet of exactly those questions: cross-system, edge-case, and "prove you understand the wiring." No new theory here — if you read Modules 8–14, you can defend all of it. Take your time on each one and read the explanation either way.

🎓
How to use this

Answer out loud as if defending live, then check. The wrong-answer explanations name the file/function you'd point the corrector to — that's the muscle you actually need on the day.

The gauntlet

Name two completely different systems that both depend on cub->zbuf.

A corrector kills two enemies, then dies on purpose. The enemies are back. Why — and what was snapshotted?

Why don't projectiles need their own projection or occlusion code?

Trace the path from picking "Gigachad" to taking more damage.

What is the actual win condition — and where is it triggered?

Reward pickups are appended to the sprite array at runtime. How is that safe without realloc?

An open door lets you walk through. Can an enemy chase you through it?

Do enemy line-of-sight and projectile collision treat doors identically?

Two enemies overlap. Two correct techniques are in play — which resolves sprite-vs-sprite?

How does spawning give the boss 8 HP and everyone else 1, without an if?

After parsing, enemy tiles vanish from the grid but weapon-pickup tiles don't. Why the difference?

Damage functions clamp hp at 0 but never trigger death. Where does dying actually happen?

Why does the world freeze during a cutscene or the death screen?

That's the whole bonus, end to end — sprites, enemies, AI, combat, HUD, game flow, and the format that drives it. If you can defend these thirteen questions, you can defend the project. Good luck at the eval.
